# Geography, Springs, Lakes and Mountains of Hot Springs / Garland County, Arkansas

Hot Springs sits in a narrow valley at the southeastern edge of the east–west Ouachita Mountains, where about four dozen thermal springs (47 is the usual count) emerge at about 143°F on the western slope of Hot Springs Mountain. The water is mostly rain that fell about 4,400 years ago, recharged through Bigfork Chert and Arkansas Novaculite and raised along thrust faults. Four big reservoirs lie south and west: Lake Ouachita (Blakely Mountain Dam, USACE, 1953), Lake Hamilton (Carpenter Dam, 1931), Lake Catherine (Remmel Dam, 1924) and DeGray Lake (USACE, 1962–72). The climate is humid subtropical (Köppen Cfa) with May the wettest month and about 1 inch of snow a year. Flow rate, Lake Ouachita acreage/depth and several elevations conflict across sources; both values are kept below.

Era ids: ancient (<1804) · frontier (1804–1874) · gilded-spa (1875–1920) · roaring (1921–1946) · midcentury (1947–1967) · revival (1968–1999) · modern (2000–2026)

## Thermal springs

### Count
- The park is centered on 47 thermal springs — [Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/place/Hot-Springs-National-Park); [Encyclopedia.com](https://www.encyclopedia.com/reference/encyclopedias-almanacs-transcripts-and-maps/hot-springs-national-park); [Wikipedia: Hot Springs, Arkansas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hot_Springs,_Arkansas) ("47 springs flow from the western slope of Hot Springs Mountain").
- conflict: 43 thermal springs are presumed to be flowing, and water from 33 of them is collected at a central reservoir — [Wikipedia: Hot Springs National Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hot_Springs_National_Park).
- NPS says "about four dozen springs" emerge at the base of Hot Springs Mountain — [NPS: Maurice Historic Display Spring](https://www.nps.gov/places/maurice-historic-display-spring.htm).
- Historical counts: Dunbar and Hunter (Dec 1804–Jan 1805) counted four principal and two lesser springs — [EOA Hunter-Dunbar](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/hunter-dunbar-expedition-2205/). 44 springs were analyzed in 1901; in 1920 there were 46 hot springs — [NPS 1920 brochure](https://npshistory.com/publications/hosp/brochures/1920/sec1.htm). Today 47 springs — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hot_Springs,_Arkansas).
- Emergence zone: a gap about 1,500 ft long and 400 ft wide, at elevations of 576 to 683 ft — [Wikipedia: HSNP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hot_Springs_National_Park). NPS gives about 460 m × 120 m — [NPS Display Spring](https://www.nps.gov/places/maurice-historic-display-spring.htm).
- By 1901 all springs were walled and covered — [NPS History & Culture](https://www.nps.gov/hosp/learn/historyculture/index.htm). When the springs were enclosed in 1901, water was diverted to form a thermal cascade — [Encyclopedia of Arkansas photo entry](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/photo-of-day/this-day-1638/). 1933: a central thermal water collection and distribution system was completed — [NPS History](https://www.nps.gov/hosp/learn/historyculture/index.htm). 1982: NPS created the Thermal Water Cascade — [EOA HSNP](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/hot-springs-national-park-2547/).

### Age of the water and dating
- Scientists in the 1970s estimated a water particle takes over 4,000 years to fall as rain, travel through the rock, heat at depth and return — [NPS Display Spring](https://www.nps.gov/places/maurice-historic-display-spring.htm).
- USGS Professional Paper 1044-C: tritium and carbon-14 data show "a very small amount of water less than 20 years old and a preponderance of water about 4,400 years old." Virtually all of the water is local meteoric water (rain) — [USGS PP 1044-C abstract (NPS History)](https://www.nps.gov/parkhistory/online_books/geology/publications/pp/1044-C/abstract.htm).
- A 2008 GSA/USGS abstract cites an earlier estimate of about 4,500 years, noting it used only measured 14C and DIC with 13C assumed, and proposes a refined 14C + 13C model — [GSA 2008 South-Central abstract](https://gsa.confex.com/gsa/2008SC/webprogram/Paper136416.html).
- Radiocarbon dating puts the rain about 4,400 years before emergence — [Wikipedia: Hot Springs, AR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hot_Springs,_Arkansas).
- Depth and transit: water sinks to roughly 4,500–7,500 ft (1,400–2,300 m) and, once heated, rises in about a year — [Wikipedia: HSNP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hot_Springs_National_Park). NPS: roughly a mile beneath Hot Springs Mountain — [NPS Display Spring](https://www.nps.gov/places/maurice-historic-display-spring.htm).
- Dunbar and Hunter could not determine the water's source (1804–05) — [EOA Hunter-Dunbar](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/hunter-dunbar-expedition-2205/).

### Recharge, faults and geology
- Recharge zones are the Bigfork Chert and Arkansas Novaculite outcrops; rain infiltrates and moves slowly to depth where hot rock heats it — [USGS PP 1044-C](https://www.nps.gov/parkhistory/online_books/geology/publications/pp/1044-C/abstract.htm).
- Novaculite forms the ridges on either side of the valley. Water seeps through cracks in the novaculite and a lower chert layer. Two thrust faults, "alpha" and "beta," bring it up: alpha roughly follows Central Avenue and Fountain Street; beta follows the top edge of Hot Springs Mountain. Easily fractured sandstone outcrops near the openings let the water through — [NPS Display Spring](https://www.nps.gov/places/maurice-historic-display-spring.htm).
- Formations: Stanley Shale, Hot Springs Sandstone, Arkansas Novaculite, Polk Creek Shale, Missouri Mountain Shale, Bigfork Chert, Womble Shale. On Hot Springs Mountain the lower novaculite member is about 275 ft thick, the middle about 10 ft, the upper up to about 180 ft. The springs emerge from an overturned anticline; Wikipedia says folding happened about 500 million years ago. Northern bounding thrust fault about 9,200 ft long, dipping about 26° north; southern about 9,000 ft long, dipping about 44° north — [Wikipedia: HSNP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hot_Springs_National_Park).
- Cretaceous igneous rocks occur about 6 and 11 miles SE, at Potash Sulphur Spring and Magnet Cove — [Wikipedia: HSNP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hot_Springs_National_Park).
- **[inferred]** Puzzle/tutorial idea: rain on chert/novaculite ridges → deep heating → faults as "pipes" up to Central Avenue.

### Chemistry and radioactivity
- Mildly alkaline. Main constituents: bicarbonate 130.0 ppm, silica 53.0 ppm, calcium 47.0 ppm. Radioactivity comes mainly from dissolved radon with some radium; Wikipedia compares bathing exposure to sitting in the sun for the same time — [Wikipedia: HSNP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hot_Springs_National_Park).
- 1904: Bertram Boltwood found the waters radioactive (radium emanation) `gilded-spa` — [NPS 1920 brochure](https://npshistory.com/publications/hosp/brochures/1920/sec1.htm).
- Minerals precipitate as white-to-tan travertine ("tufa") near the openings — [Wikipedia: HSNP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hot_Springs_National_Park).
- The high temperature kills most harmful bacteria; water is monitored to US drinking-water standards. Legionella has been found in some samples; the concern is inhalation, not drinking — [NPS: Experience the Water](https://www.nps.gov/hosp/planyourvisit/thermal-springs.htm).
- Dunbar and Hunter described microorganisms living in the hot water (possibly the first such report) — [EOA Hunter-Dunbar](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/hunter-dunbar-expedition-2205/).
- Most hot spring water elsewhere is not potable (sulfur, bacteria, algae), per brewer Rose Schweikhart — [All About Beer](https://allaboutbeer.com/superior-bathhouse-brewery/).

### Hot Springs Creek and the arch
- Hot Springs Creek rises near Whittington Avenue, runs in a tunnel under Bathhouse Row (Central Ave.), surfaces south of the Row and empties into Lake Hamilton — [Wikipedia: Hot Springs, AR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hot_Springs,_Arkansas).
- Enclosed in an underground arch 1882–1883 `gilded-spa` — [NPS History & Culture](https://www.nps.gov/hosp/learn/historyculture/index.htm). conflict: some NPS summaries give 1882–84 (see Caveats); the NHL nomination gives 1884 and the CLI "completed 1886" — [NHL nomination](https://npgallery.nps.gov/NRHP/GetAsset/NHLS/74000275_text); [CLI](https://npshistory.com/publications/hosp/cli-bathhouse-row.pdf).

### Tufa, algae and steam (visual-science facts)
- Tufa forms when thermal water evaporates at the surface, leaving carbonate deposits; locally "the color tends to be a more drab gray" — [NPS What is tufa?](https://www.nps.gov/hosp/learn/photosmultimedia/tufa.htm).
- An older account describes "the brilliant emerald-green tufa, whose color is due to the growth of hot-water algae" — [Haywood & Weed, NPS online book](https://www.nps.gov/parkhistory/online_books/hosp/haywood-weed/sec9.htm).
- A blue-green alga, *Phormidium treleasei*, grows where spring water meets light and air — [Encyclopedia of Arkansas photo entry](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/photo-of-day/this-day-1638/).
- Tufa Terrace Trail: 0.3 mi, paved, 6% grade, 650–745 ft; lower section near the Hot Water Cascade — [NPS: Hot Springs & North Mountain Trails](https://www.nps.gov/hosp/planyourvisit/hot-springs-and-north-mountain-trails.htm).
- The nickname "Valley of Vapors" comes from the steam rising off the springs — [npshistory.com (via search summary)](https://www.npshistory.com/publications/hosp/index.htm).

## Rocks and minerals

### Novaculite
- Hard, dense, white-to-gray-black rock of microcrystalline quartz; name from Latin for "razor stone"; conchoidal fracture; formations in the Ouachitas about 250–900 ft thick — [EOA Novaculite](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/novaculite-2220/).
- Sedimentary rock mostly of cryptocrystalline (1–5 micron) quartz, a recrystallized chert, known worldwide for whetstones and oilstones — [Arkansas Geological Survey](https://geology.arkansas.gov/minerals/industrial/novaculite-silica-stone.html). "A dense, hard, recrystallized chert" from white to grayish-black; resists erosion and forms prominent ridges; abrasive stone mined mostly in Garland and Hot Spring counties and sold as "Arkansas" and "Washita" stones — [Arkansas Geological Survey](https://www.geology.arkansas.gov/minerals/industrial/novaculite-silica-stone.html).
- Colors: often white or light gray, also pink, red, tan, dark gray and black. Found only in the Ouachitas (AR/OK) and southwest Texas. More than 120 prehistoric quarries recorded in Arkansas — [Arkansas Archeological Survey PDF](https://archeology.uark.edu/wp-content/uploads/2021/07/Arkansas-Novaculite.pdf).
- Grades: "Washita" (dull, porous, like unglazed porcelain) and "Arkansas" (very fine-grained, waxy, denser). Top whetstone-grade novaculite is mined almost only in Garland and Hot Spring counties — [EOA Novaculite](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/novaculite-2220/).
- Ancient quarries (Shinn, 1905 textbook): some running about 4 miles; pits 15–40 ft deep, 10–30 ft wide, 100–300 ft long; flakes covering hundreds of square miles `ancient` — [EOA Novaculite](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/novaculite-2220/).
- Peoples listed as novaculite miners: Quapaw, Osage, Caddo, Tunica, Chickasaw, Natchez (no dates or per-group evidence; treat as general); traded over much of North America — [EOA Novaculite](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/novaculite-2220/).
- "Called Arkansas's oldest industry"; French trappers sharpened skinning knives with it; settlers sharpened axes/saws and traded it — [EOA Novaculite](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/novaculite-2220/).
- Whetstone mining began as early as 1818 in the Hot Springs region; commercial mining concentrated in Garland and Hot Spring counties — [Arkansas Archeological Survey PDF](https://archeology.uark.edu/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/ArkansasNovaculite-rev_acc.pdf).
- The Dunbar–Hunter party found the whetstone rock in 1804; deposits became the main American whetstone source until artificial whetstones in the 1970s — [EOA Hunter-Dunbar (via search summary)](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/hunter-dunbar-expedition-2205/).
- 1920s–30s novaculite mining added to the county economy `roaring` — [EOA Garland County](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/garland-county-770/).
- Park archaeology: 92 known sites "and counting," including prehistoric novaculite quarries; artifacts include novaculite projectile points and grinding stones — [NPS Archeology](https://www.nps.gov/hosp/learn/historyculture/archeology.htm).

### Quartz crystal and Mount Ida
- Quartz crystals formed after the Ouachita Orogeny, especially around Mount Ida — [Wikipedia: Ouachita Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ouachita_Mountains).
- Mount Ida (Montgomery County seat): motto "Quartz Crystal Capital of the World," elevation 659 ft, about 36 miles west of Hot Springs via US 270 — [Wikipedia: Mount Ida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Ida,_Arkansas). Public dig-your-own mines; hosts the World Championship Quartz Crystal Dig; quartz crystal became the state mineral in 1976 — [Arkansas.com: Mount Ida](https://www.arkansas.com/articles/mount-ida-home-crystals-and-more).
- Lake Ouachita contains "one of the largest crystal veins in the world" and a marked Geo-Float Trail for boaters — [Wikipedia: Lake Ouachita](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Ouachita).

## Mountains and terrain
- The Ouachitas trend "mostly east-west," unlike most US ranges. Fold-and-thrust belt outcropping for about 220 miles across western Arkansas and SE Oklahoma, in highly deformed Paleozoic (Cambrian–Pennsylvanian) strata. Wikipedia names the highest peak as Mount Magazine, 2,753 ft. The Zig Zag Mountains in Garland County hold the park's springs — [Wikipedia: Ouachita Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ouachita_Mountains).
- Hot Springs city: elevation 568 ft, area 37.57 sq mi, coordinates 34°30′13″N 93°3′19″W, at the SE edge of the Ouachitas — [Wikipedia: Hot Springs, AR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hot_Springs,_Arkansas). conflict: EOA gives 37.47 sq mi — [EOA Hot Springs (city)](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/hot-springs-887/). The city sits around 600 ft, with surrounding hills about 600 ft higher — [Wikipedia: HSNP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hot_Springs_National_Park).
- Park area: 5,550 acres (infobox) vs. 5,839 acres after expansion (history section) (conflict). Includes North, West and Sugarloaf mountains and Whittington Lake Park — [Wikipedia: HSNP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hot_Springs_National_Park). Historical: 1832 reservation 2,529.1 acres — [EOA HSNP](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/hot-springs-national-park-2547/); by 1920, 911.63 acres (Hot Springs, North and West mountains and Whittington Lake Park) with more than 11 miles of roads and paths — [NPS 1920 brochure](https://npshistory.com/publications/hosp/brochures/1920/sec1.htm); reservation 264.93 acres and townsite of 196 blocks / ~50 miles of streets after the 1876 ruling — [Wikipedia: Hot Springs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hot_Springs,_Arkansas). In June 1880 Congress added the remaining mountain lands — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hot_Springs,_Arkansas).

## Rivers
- Ouachita River: 605 miles, rising in Polk County, AR (34°41′56″N 94°19′57″W), joining the Tensas near Jonesville, LA, to form the Black River. Blakely Mountain Dam (Lake Ouachita), Carpenter Dam (Lake Hamilton) and Lake Catherine (Remmel Dam) impound it in sequence. Rainbow trout fishing is popular in the tailwaters of Lakes Ouachita, Hamilton and Catherine. Fish include black bass, white bass, bream, freshwater drum and gar. Some areas have mercury advisories — [Wikipedia: Ouachita River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ouachita_River).
- 1804: the Dunbar–Hunter party ascended the Ouachita, passed the Chutes rapids Dec. 3, camped at Calfait (Gulpha) Creek Dec. 7 — [EOA Hunter-Dunbar](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/hunter-dunbar-expedition-2205/). Mounds over burned buildings (14th–15th c.) were found along the Ouachita by M.R. Harrington, with occupation remains about 17 ft deep at one riverside camp — [EOA Garland County](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/garland-county-770/).
- Caddo River feeds DeGray Lake; Alum Fork of the Saline feeds Lake Winona (above). The Watson and then Ross families ran a water-powered corn mill on the South Fork of the Saline River (Whittington area) — [EOA Whittington (via search summary)](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/whittington-garland-county-10724/).
- French place names such as Fourche a Loupe point to 18th-century French presence — [EOA Garland County](https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/garland-county-770/).

## Game hooks
All **[inferred]** design ideas from the notes' Game-hooks sections; mechanics are original.
1. **Thermal water jug run (farming/life sim):** fill jugs at Reserve St. fountains (hot, 143°F) or Happy Hollow/Whittington (cold); hot water speeds crafting/cooking, cold water for drinking or crops.
2. **4,000-year water timer:** rain that falls "today" in-game emerges 4,400 years later; tritium/C-14 "lab" minigame.
3. **Fault-line tutorial:** rain on novaculite/chert ridges → heated ~1 mile down → alpha/beta faults lift it to Central Avenue.
4. **Hiking stamina by real NPS grades:** Short Cut (21.1%) drains stamina fastest; Hot Springs Mountain Trail (0.5%) nearly flat; Music Mountain (1,405 ft) summit achievement.
5. **Tower view unlock:** climbing the 216-ft tower clears fog-of-war, nod to the old tower's "120 mile view."
6. **Island navigation (Ouachita):** ~200 islands and numbered island markers as waypoints, buoy-marker minimap.
7. **Geo-Float Trail side quest:** boat between geology stops to find the giant quartz vein (crystal collectible).
8. **Quartz Crystal Dig (Mount Ida):** annual dig-competition minigame (real event month not sourced—verify).
9. **Novaculite whetstone crafting:** mine "Washita" (common) and "Arkansas" (rare) grades; sharper hooks/knives = better results; quarry pits 15–40 ft deep.
10. **Follow the river campaign:** Ouachita → Hamilton → Catherine, each dam a level gate; Blakely flooding Buckville and Cedar Glades as a relocation event.
11. **Weather from real normals:** May storms (wettest) bring muddy water; August 93°F heat drives fish deep and drains stamina; rare snow day closes ramps.
12. **Flood events:** 1923 (manholes blown up), May 1990 (13 in., pumps contaminated, fountains shut), May 2020 (85 rescued).
13. **Hot Springs Creek tunnel dungeon:** creek runs under Bathhouse Row and drains to Lake Hamilton—secret passage from downtown to the lake.
14. **Ral City / free spring encampment** as a charity subplot; Display Spring as an open-air "natural state" exhibit.
15. **Steam valley visuals:** steam curls, gray tufa terraces with emerald/blue-green algae fringe; "Valley of Vapors" fog on cold mornings (lake fog itself unverified).

## Caveats
- **Spring count:** 47 (Britannica, Encyclopedia.com, Wikipedia city page) vs. 43 presumed flowing / 33 collected (Wikipedia HSNP) vs. "about four dozen" (NPS); 46 in 1920; 44 analyzed 1901; Dunbar–Hunter counted 4 principal + 2 lesser.
- **Flow rate conflict:** 700,000+ (NPS), 750,000–950,000 and "well over a half million" (Wikipedia HSNP), ~1,000,000 (Wikipedia city), 850,000+ (Britannica), 1M+ (Encyclopedia.com), 851,308 (1920). Keep out of trivia or frame with "about."
- **Water age:** ~4,400 years (USGS PP 1044-C) vs. earlier ~4,500 estimate (GSA 2008 notes uncertainty) vs. "over 4,000" (NPS). The publication year of USGS PP 1044-C was not shown (believed 1979, unverified).
- **Creek arch dates:** 1882–83 vs. 1882–84 differ slightly between NPS summaries; the NHL nomination says 1884 and the CLI "completed 1886"; "10-year flood" design is (via search summary). The ~6,100-ft culvert length and route are OSM-derived.
- **Summit elevations:** Hot Springs Mountain now rests on a USGS EPQS point query (1,036 ft at the tower); West Mountain (~1,419 ft) and Sugarloaf (~1,129 ft) are EPQS values at OSM peak nodes, derived by game sessions, not published summit elevations. North Mountain is still **[inferred]** from trail ranges.
- **Named creeks** are NHD features whose mouths fall inside the fishing game's map frames; the list is not a complete inventory of each lake's tributaries.
- **Music Mountain:** 1,405 ft (NPS) vs. 1,418 ft (SummitPost). No official (published) summit elevations for West, North, Sugarloaf or Indian mountains; Indian Mountain not found in any source. Hot Springs Mountain now has a USGS EPQS ground elevation at the tower (1,036 ft; tower top ~1,252 ft), and West (~1,419 ft) and Sugarloaf (~1,129 ft) have EPQS values at OSM peak nodes [derived]; North Mountain's height is still **[inferred]** from trail ranges.
- **Park acreage:** 5,550 vs. 5,839 acres (Wikipedia internal conflict). City area 37.57 (Wikipedia) vs. 37.47 sq mi (EOA).
- **Lake Ouachita:** 40,000 vs. "over 66,324" acres; max depth 406 ft is suspect (dam only 231 ft); avg depth 50 vs. 51 ft; "975 miles" shoreline unverified; "largest lake wholly in Arkansas" is citation-needed; impoundment year (1953 completion vs. 1952/1955 filling) unconfirmed; Trophic State Index 37 is from a search summary with uncertain host page.
- **Lake Hamilton** shoreline (~80 mi) and max depth (~57 ft) are **[UNVERIFIED]**; no primary Entergy page fetched for Hamilton/Catherine shoreline or depth.
- **DeGray** acreage 13,800 / 13,400 / 13,420; DeGray state park 984 vs. 938 acres.
- **HSV lakes:** Coronado 380 vs. ~360 acres. **Lake Winona** water share 35% vs. ~40%.

- **Climate:** annual precip 52.87 (climate-charts) vs. 57.96 in (Wikipedia); May 6.3 vs. 6.51; climate-charts table labeled "NOAA Monthly Normals 2006-2020" though the page header cites 1991–2020 **[DISPUTED]** period label (the football session hit the same label; check NOAA NCEI 1991–2020 normals for station USW00003962 before quoting exact numbers — `games/football/research/stadiums.md` §5); NationalParked table period unstated.
